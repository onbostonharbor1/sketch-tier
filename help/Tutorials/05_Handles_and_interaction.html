Handles are the red dots you drag. One controller, the interactor, owns the mouse on the canvas for one armed object at a time. This tutorial follows a drag from the mouse to the redraw, the arrow-key nudge, handles under a figure layer's transform, and how objects react.

5.1 The interactor: arming, hit testing, dragging

Read

ui/interactor.js: armInteractor, disarmInteractor, getCanvasCoords, moveHandle, render, refreshInteractor.

What happens

Only one object's handles are live at a time; arming hands the interactor that object's params.points and a redraw function. A mouse press is turned into canvas coordinates (getCanvasCoords, allowing for the canvas being shown larger or smaller than its pixels); if it falls within the hit radius of a handle, that handle is pressed and selected. Moving the mouse calls moveHandle(i, x, y), the single place every handle move goes through, which writes the point and asks the object to update and redraw. render draws the handles on the interaction layer.

5.2 Nudging with the arrow keys

Read

ui/interactor.js, the key handling around the selected handle.

What happens

A pressed handle stays selected. Arrow keys move it 1 pixel, 10 with Shift, through the same moveHandle, so a nudge behaves exactly like a tiny drag.

5.3 Handles under a layer transform

Read

draw/drawTransform.js (mapPoint, inverseTransform, applyTransform); ui/interactor.js (toObject, toCanvas, settings.getTransform); ui/figures/figurePoints.js (layerTransform).

What happens

In Figures a layer can be moved and rotated, but its object still draws in its own coordinates; drawLine maps every line through the layer's transform. Handles must be mapped the same way: drawn at the mapped position (toCanvas) and, when the mouse moves, the canvas position mapped back into the object's coordinates (toObject, using inverseTransform). That is why a handle stays under the mouse even on a rotated layer. Discs use the same transform (figurePoints.js).

5.4 How objects react to a moved handle

Read

Each registry object's update(): Line, Parabola, and the ring maker (draw/ringObject.js).

What happens

An object's update decides what a moved handle means. Line moves as a whole when its midpoint moves; Parabola's corner handles reshape its arms; ring objects move when the centre moves and resize when the size handle moves. In Figures, a welded handle also moves its partners (tutorial 7).
